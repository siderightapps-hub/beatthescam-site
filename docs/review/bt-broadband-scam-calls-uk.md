## 1. What a fake BT call sounds like

A BT scam call is a phone call from a criminal pretending to be BT, Openreach, or a 'broadband technical team'. A typical opener is: `Hello, this is BT technical support — we've detected a serious problem with your router and your broadband will be disconnected within 24 hours unless we fix it now.` Others offer a refund for an 'overcharge' or a 'compensation payment'. The aim is to frighten or tempt you into letting them control your computer, log into your bank, or read out codes. This guide shows what a genuine BT call will never ask, how to check safely, and what to do if you've already acted.


## 2. Why a fake BT call is so convincing

Scammers can fake the number that shows on your phone, so a call that appears to come from 'BT' or a normal-looking landline proves nothing. They often already know your name, address, or that you're a BT customer, which makes them sound legitimate. They lean on fear — your line being cut off, your router 'hacked', fraud on your account — or on the lure of a refund. A genuine BT call will never ask you to install software so they can control your device, log in to your online banking, move money to a 'safe account', or read out a one-time passcode or card details.


## 3. Signs a BT call is a scam

- It's unexpected and claims a problem with your router, line, internet speed, or a 'hacked' connection.
- It pressures you — your broadband will be cut off, or a refund will be lost — unless you act immediately.
- It asks you to install remote-access software (such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer) or visit a website so they can 'fix' your computer.
- It asks you to log in to your online banking, or offers a 'refund' that needs your bank details to process.
- The caller 'refunds too much' by mistake and asks you to send the difference back — the money was never really theirs to refund.
- It asks for card details, your full password, or a one-time passcode.
- Errors or a poor line can be clues, but a confident, polished caller can still be a scammer.


## 4. How the BT scam call works

First, an unexpected call claims a fault with your broadband, or offers a refund. Second, the caller builds pressure or trust and asks to 'fix' the problem by having you install remote-access software, or to 'process the refund' through your online banking. Third, once they can see your screen or you've logged in, they show you a fake 'refund' that looks too large, or move money themselves. Fourth, they ask you to repay the 'overpayment', or read out a passcode that approves a real payment. Finally, your money is gone and they may keep access to your device. Hanging up at the first unexpected call is the safest move.


## 5. How to check if a BT call is genuine

Don't act on anything the caller tells you in the moment.
- Hang up. A genuine company won't mind you calling back, and won't pressure you.
- Never install software, visit a website, or grant remote access because an unexpected caller asked.
- Wait a few minutes (or use a different phone) so the line clears, then call BT on the number on your bill or at bt.com.
- If anything touched your bank — a 'refund', a login, a transfer — call your bank straight away, or use 159, a secure service that connects you safely to your bank.
If you're unsure whether a website the caller sent is genuine, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/isp-impersonation-scam-bt-sky-virgin-media/ guide covers the same trick from other providers.


## 6. If you let them in or shared details

Act quickly. If you installed remote-access software or let the caller control your device, disconnect from the internet, then run a security scan and remove the software; change important passwords from a different, trusted device. If you logged in to your bank, moved money, or shared card details or a passcode, contact your bank immediately — use 159 or the number on your card — and ask them to secure your account and check for payments you didn't make. If you sent money, report it as fraud at once — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep any notes about the call as evidence.


## 7. How to report a BT scam call (UK)

Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. Tell BT about the call through their official website or the number on your bill, so they can warn other customers. If the same scam reached you by text, forward it to 7726. For free, independent advice you can also contact the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133. Note down the date, time, and what was said.


## FAQ

**Does BT call its customers?**

Yes, BT does sometimes call customers — so a call being from 'BT' isn't automatically a scam. But a genuine BT call will never ask you to install remote-access software, log in to your online banking, move money to a 'safe account', or read out a passcode or card details. If a caller asks for any of that, hang up and call BT back on the number on your bill.

**Can BT really cut off my broadband if I don't act now?**

No — that urgency is the scam. A genuine provider won't threaten to disconnect you within minutes or hours unless you act on a phone call. Take your time, hang up, and call BT on the number on your bill to check.

**I let a 'BT' caller take control of my computer — what now?**

Disconnect from the internet, run a security scan, and remove any remote-access software they had you install. Change your important passwords from a different, trusted device. If you logged in to your bank or shared card details, call your bank straight away on 159 or the number on your card and ask them to secure your account.

**The caller 'refunded' me too much and wants the difference back — is it a scam?**

Yes. This is a classic refund scam: the 'overpayment' is faked (often by moving your own money between your accounts so it looks like a credit), and the money you 'send back' is real and goes to the criminal. Don't repay anything; hang up and call your bank on 159 or the number on your card.

**How do I report a BT scam call?**

Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101), and tell BT through their official website. If you lost money, contact your bank immediately.
