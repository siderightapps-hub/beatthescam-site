## 1. Is Wish a scam, or just cheap?

Wish is a real, well-known shopping app — not itself a scam — but it's a high-risk place to shop, and the genuine dangers are counterfeit goods, items that never arrive, poor quality versus the photos, and phishing emails that impersonate Wish. A typical phishing message reads: `Wish: there's a problem with your order. Confirm your payment details here: wish-order-verify.example`. So 'Wish scam' usually means one of three things: a misleading or counterfeit listing, an order that doesn't turn up, or a fake 'Wish' email after your details. This guide explains what to watch for and how to buy more safely.


## 2. Why Wish shopping goes wrong

Wish lists huge numbers of ultra-cheap items shipped from overseas sellers, so prices are low but quality, accuracy, and delivery times vary a lot. Branded items at a fraction of the real price are usually counterfeit or never arrive. Long shipping windows make it harder to spot a non-delivery early, and the volume of sellers makes vetting difficult. Separately, scammers send phishing emails dressed up as Wish order or refund notices to capture your card or login details — these have nothing to do with a real order, and Wish won't ask for your payment details through an email link.


## 3. Signs of a Wish scam or bad buy

- A branded item is priced far below its normal retail price — usually counterfeit or a non-delivery.
- A listing uses only stock photos, vague descriptions, or has no genuine reviews.
- A 'Wish' email asks you to confirm card details, your password, or bank information through a link.
- The email or message link isn't a genuine Wish address — it's a lookalike or shortened link.
- A seller asks you to pay or continue outside the Wish app.
- You're pressured to buy immediately before a 'deal' ends.
- Errors can be clues, but a polished listing or email can still be a scam.


## 4. How Wish problems and scams work

With listings, the risk is simple: a too-good price draws an impulse buy, then the item arrives as a poor counterfeit, looks nothing like the photo, or never comes at all — and a long shipping window delays you noticing. With phishing, a fake 'Wish' email about an order, a refund, or a payment problem links you to a copycat page that captures your card or login details. The details are then used for fraud or sold on. Paying only through the app, and checking orders in the app rather than via an email link, avoids most of it.


## 5. How to buy more safely on Wish

Shop with your eyes open and keep payment in the app.
- Pay only through the Wish app, so its refund and buyer-protection process can apply; never pay a seller outside it.
- Treat branded goods at tiny prices as fake, and read genuine reviews before buying.
- Check an order's real status in the app, not through a link in an email.
- Ignore 'Wish' emails that ask for card or login details — Wish won't ask for those by email link.
- Use a card rather than a bank transfer, for the extra protection it gives.

## 6. If you've paid or shared details

If an item hasn't arrived or is counterfeit, raise it through the Wish app's refund or dispute process first, then ask your card issuer about a chargeback if that fails. If you entered card or bank details on a fake 'Wish' page, contact your bank using the number on your card, tell them it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card. If you sent a bank transfer, report it as fraud — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk if you shared personal details, and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the listing, order number, and any emails.


## 7. How to report a Wish scam (UK)

Report a counterfeit listing, a seller, or a problem order through the Wish app. If you got a phishing email pretending to be Wish, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk; if it came by text, forward it to 7726. If you lost money, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101.


## FAQ

**Is Wish a legitimate company or a scam?**

Wish is a real, legitimate shopping app — it's not itself a scam. But it's a high-risk marketplace: counterfeit goods, items that don't arrive, and quality far below the photos are common, and scammers also send phishing emails impersonating Wish. Buy carefully, pay in the app, and treat impossibly cheap branded items as fake.

**Why is Wish so cheap?**

Most items ship directly from overseas sellers with very low costs and long delivery times, which is why prices are low — but it's also why quality and accuracy vary and branded 'bargains' are usually counterfeit or never arrive. Cheap doesn't always mean a scam, but it does mean buy with low expectations and pay through the app.

**My Wish order never arrived — can I get a refund?**

Often, yes. Raise it through the Wish app's refund or dispute process first, within any time limits. If that fails and you paid by card, ask your card issuer about a chargeback. Keep your order number and any messages as evidence.

**I got a 'Wish' email asking for my card details — is it genuine?**

No. Wish won't ask you to confirm card details, your password, or bank information through an email link. Treat it as phishing: don't click the link, check any real order in the app, and forward the email to report@phishing.gov.uk.

**How do I report a Wish scam?**

Report a bad listing or seller through the Wish app. Forward phishing 'Wish' emails to report@phishing.gov.uk. If you lost money, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101).
