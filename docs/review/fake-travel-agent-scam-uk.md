## 1. What is a fake travel agent scam?

A fake travel agent scam happens when criminals set up fraudulent travel booking websites, social media accounts, or email addresses that mimic real travel companies or pose as independent agents. They advertise heavily discounted holidays, flights, or package deals to lure UK customers into paying upfront. Once payment is made—usually by bank transfer, debit card, or credit card—the scammer either disappears entirely, sends fake booking confirmations, or delivers nothing at all. Some variants involve the scammer requesting personal information (passport details, address, date of birth) under the guise of completing a booking, which is then used for identity theft or sold to other criminals. The scam exploits the fact that many people book travel online without meeting an agent face-to-face, making it easy for fraudsters to operate without detection.


## 2. Warning signs of a fake travel agent

- Prices significantly lower than competitors for the same flight, hotel, or package—if it looks too cheap, it almost certainly is.
- Website URL that is slightly different from the real company (e.g. 'travelagent-uk.com' instead of 'travelagent.co.uk'), or a domain registered very recently.
- No phone number listed, or a number that goes unanswered; legitimate travel agents always provide direct contact details.
- Poor spelling, grammar, or unprofessional design on the website or in emails—real travel companies invest in quality.
- Pressure to pay immediately or book 'today only' to secure a deal; scammers rush you to prevent verification.
- Payment methods limited to bank transfer only, or requests to pay via unusual channels like cryptocurrency or gift cards.
- No verifiable reviews on independent sites like Trustpilot or Google; fake agents either have no reviews or suspiciously perfect ones.
- Inability to find the company registered with ABTA (Association of British Travel Agents) or ATOL (Air Travel Organisers' Licensing).


## 3. How the scam unfolds from first contact to loss

The scam typically begins with an advert on social media (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok), a search engine result, or an unsolicited email offering an irresistible holiday deal. The victim clicks through to what appears to be a professional travel booking website. The site looks legitimate—it may copy the design of a real travel company or use professional-looking graphics and testimonials. The victim selects their flights, hotel, and dates, then proceeds to checkout. At payment, they are asked to transfer money to a bank account, often with a name that does not match the company, or to pay via debit/credit card on a form that may not be encrypted. Once payment is sent, one of several things happens: the scammer stops responding to emails and messages; a fake booking confirmation is sent with a reference number that does not work when checked with the airline or hotel; or the victim receives nothing and discovers the website has been taken down. By this point, the money is gone and the scammer has the victim's personal and financial details.


## 4. How to verify a travel agent is genuine before you book

Before entering any payment details, take these steps to confirm legitimacy. First, check if the company is registered with ABTA or holds an ATOL licence—both organisations maintain searchable registers on their websites. If the agent claims to represent a major airline or hotel chain, go directly to that company's official website (type the URL yourself, do not click a link from the email or ad) and check their list of approved partners. Call the travel company using the phone number on their official website, not any number provided in an email or advert. Ask them to confirm your booking details verbally before you pay. Search the company name plus 'scam' or 'reviews' on Google and Trustpilot; if there are multiple complaints about non-delivery or fake confirmations, walk away. Check the website's security: the URL should start with 'https://' (not 'http://'), and there should be a padlock icon in the address bar. Finally, if you are booking through social media, be extremely cautious—legitimate travel agents rarely conduct full transactions via Facebook Messenger or Instagram DM.


## 5. What to do immediately if you have already paid a fake agent

Act quickly. If you paid by debit or credit card, contact your bank or card issuer right away and report the transaction as fraudulent. Most card providers can freeze the payment or initiate a chargeback within 30 days. If you made a bank transfer, contact your bank immediately and ask them to recall the payment; success depends on how quickly you act, but banks can sometimes reverse transfers within hours. Do not send any more money, even if the scammer claims there is a 'processing fee' or 'tax' to release your booking. Change your passwords for any online accounts you used during the booking, especially email and banking apps. If you provided your passport number, date of birth, or full address, monitor your credit file using a free service like Clearscore or Experian's free tier to watch for fraudulent accounts opened in your name. Report the scam to Action Fraud (see below) and provide them with all correspondence, payment details, and the website URL.


## 6. Reporting a fake travel agent scam in the UK

Report the scam to Action Fraud by calling 0300 123 2040 (Monday to Friday, 8am to 8pm) or online at actionfraud.police.uk. Have ready: the website URL, the scammer's email address, the bank account details you were asked to pay, and copies of all emails and messages. If you received the scam via email, forward it to the NCSC Suspicious Email Reporting Service at report@phishing.gov.uk. If it came as a text or WhatsApp message, forward it to 7726 (Spam). Report the fake website to the Internet Watch Foundation at iwf.org.uk if it is hosting illegal content or impersonating a real company. If the scammer impersonated a specific travel company, report it directly to that company's fraud team—they can take action against the fake website and warn other customers. Contact Citizens Advice on 0808 223 1133 if you need support understanding your rights or next steps. Keep all evidence (screenshots, emails, transaction records) in case your bank or the police need it for a chargeback or investigation.


## FAQ

**How do I know if a travel website is real or fake?**

Check the website's URL carefully—scammers often use URLs that are one letter off from the real company. Verify the company is registered with ABTA or ATOL using their official registers. Call the company directly using the number on their official website (not from the email or advert) to confirm your booking before paying. If the website has poor design, spelling errors, or no contact phone number, it is almost certainly fake.

**I paid a fake travel agent by bank transfer—can I get my money back?**

Contact your bank immediately and report the payment as fraudulent. Banks can sometimes recall transfers within hours if the receiving account has not yet withdrawn the money, but success depends on speed. If the recall fails, ask your bank about a chargeback claim, though this is more difficult with bank transfers than with card payments. Report the scam to Action Fraud (0300 123 2040) and provide them with all transaction details; they may be able to help trace the account.

**What should I do if I gave a fake travel agent my passport details?**

Monitor your credit file immediately using a free service like Clearscore or Experian to watch for fraudulent accounts opened in your name. Consider placing a fraud alert with Experian or Equifax. Report the scam to Action Fraud and provide details of what information was shared. You may also want to contact your passport office to check if anyone has tried to renew or replace your passport fraudulently. Change your passwords for email and banking accounts.

**Can I trust travel deals advertised on social media?**

Be very cautious. Scammers use Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok to advertise fake travel deals because the platforms are easy to set up and hard to monitor. Legitimate travel companies do advertise on social media, but they will always direct you to their official website and provide verifiable contact details. Never complete a booking or payment via social media messenger—real agents will ask you to visit their website or call them. If a deal seems unusually cheap, verify the company independently before clicking any links.

**What is the difference between a fake travel agent and a legitimate discount travel site?**

Legitimate discount travel sites (like Skyscanner, Kayak, or Booking.com) are registered companies with real phone support, transparent pricing, and customer reviews on independent platforms. They do not ask for bank transfers and always use encrypted payment pages. Fake agents often have no phone number, pressure you to pay immediately, offer prices that are unrealistically low, and disappear after payment. If you cannot find independent reviews or verify the company's registration, do not book.
