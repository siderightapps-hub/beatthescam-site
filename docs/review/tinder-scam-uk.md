## 1. What a Tinder scam looks like

A Tinder scam is a romance scam: someone creates a fake profile, builds a connection, and then exploits it for money or details. The pattern is recognisable — a match who seems perfect, moves the chat off Tinder to WhatsApp or Telegram quickly, talks about strong feelings within days, and always has a reason they can't meet or video-call. Then comes the ask: a sudden emergency (a medical bill, being 'stuck' abroad, a customs fee), or a 'great' crypto or investment opportunity they want to share with you. Anyone can be targeted — these criminals are practised and patient. This guide shows the signs, how to protect yourself, and how to report it.


## 2. Why romance scams are so convincing

Romance scammers invest time — sometimes weeks — building trust before they ask for anything, which is exactly what makes it hard to spot. They use attractive stolen photos, claim jobs that explain why they're far away or can't meet (working overseas, on an oil rig, in the military, a doctor on a mission), and mirror your interests. The emotional connection is real to you, even though the person isn't who they claim. That bond is the tool: by the time money is mentioned, refusing can feel like letting down someone you care about. Recognising the pattern early — not judging yourself — is the protection.


## 3. Signs a Tinder match is a scammer

- They move the conversation off Tinder to WhatsApp or Telegram very quickly.
- They profess strong feelings unusually fast, before you've really met.
- They always have a reason they can't meet in person or do a live video call.
- Their story explains the distance — working abroad, military deployment, an oil rig, a contract overseas.
- Eventually they ask for money — an emergency, travel, customs, or medical costs — usually by bank transfer, gift cards, or cryptocurrency.
- Or they steer you toward a crypto or investment 'opportunity' with guaranteed returns (a 'pig-butchering' scam).
- They ask for intimate photos, which can later be used to threaten you.
- Details don't add up, or their photos appear elsewhere online under different names.


## 4. How the romance scam works

First, a fake profile matches with you and quickly moves the chat to a private app. Second, over days or weeks they build a strong emotional bond and avoid meeting or video-calling. Third, a crisis appears, or an investment 'opportunity' is dangled. Fourth, they ask for money — often by transfer, gift card, or crypto, which is hard to trace and recover. Fifth, once you pay, the asks usually continue, with new emergencies, until you stop. The money rarely comes back, so the safest point to act is before any money or crypto changes hands.


## 5. How to protect yourself

Keep your guard up until you've genuinely verified who you're talking to.
- Keep chatting within Tinder until you've met in person or had a live video call you're sure is real.
- Do a reverse-image search of their photos to see if they appear elsewhere under other names.
- Never send money, gift cards, or cryptocurrency to someone you haven't met in person — no matter the story.
- Be very wary of any investment or crypto 'tip' from a romantic interest; promised guaranteed returns are a scam.
- Insist on a live video call, and notice if they always have an excuse.
If a link or 'investment platform' they sent looks doubtful, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/bumble-romance-scam-uk/ guide covers the same pattern on other apps.


## 6. If you've sent money or shared details

First, this is not your fault — these criminals are skilled, and feeling embarrassed is exactly what stops people reporting. If you sent money by bank transfer, contact your bank immediately — use the number on your card — and report it as fraud; since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Money sent by cryptocurrency or gift card is much harder to recover, so report it quickly and stop any further payments. Block the profile and keep the messages. If you shared personal details, consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk. If you shared intimate images and are being threatened, do not pay — that is sextortion; our /guides/sextortion-email-scam-uk/ guide explains what to do.


## 7. How to report a Tinder scam (UK)

Report and block the profile inside Tinder (use the Report option on their profile or chat), so the platform can remove it. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. For free, confidential support and advice, contact the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133. Keep screenshots of the profile and your conversations as evidence.


## FAQ

**Is Tinder safe, or is everyone on it a scammer?**

Tinder is a genuine app and most people use it honestly — but romance scammers do operate on every dating platform. The app being real doesn't make every match genuine, so watch for the pattern: moving off the app fast, avoiding video calls, and eventually asking for money or pushing an investment.

**My match needs money for an emergency — should I send it?**

No. Asking for money — for an emergency, travel, customs, or medical costs — is the core of a romance scam, especially from someone you haven't met in person. Don't send money, gift cards, or crypto, however convincing or urgent it feels. A genuine partner you've never met won't ask.

**My match suggested a crypto or investment opportunity — is that a scam?**

Almost certainly. A romantic interest steering you toward crypto or an 'investment platform' with guaranteed returns is a well-known scam (sometimes called 'pig butchering'). The platform is fake and any 'profits' you see are bait. Don't invest, and stop sending money.

**I've already sent money — can I get it back?**

Possibly. Tell your bank immediately. For UK bank transfers, since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Money sent by cryptocurrency or gift card is much harder to recover, so report it quickly. Keep all the messages and report it to Report Fraud.

**How do I report a Tinder scam, and who first?**

Report and block the profile in Tinder first so it can be removed, then report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). If you sent money, tell your bank straight away.
