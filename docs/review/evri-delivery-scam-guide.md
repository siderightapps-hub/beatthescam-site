## 1. What a fake Evri text looks like

An Evri parcel scam text is a smishing message that imitates the courier so you tap a link and hand over card or personal details. A typical one reads something like: `Evri: we missed you. Reschedule delivery and pay the £1.79 fee: evri-reschedule.example`. The hook is a small, believable fee — a reschedule, a 'release', an address check — because a low amount lowers your guard while the real target is your card number. The link opens a fake page that captures whatever you type. This guide shows how to spot the fake, track your parcel safely, and report it.


## 2. Why Evri texts are easy to fake (and the Hermes link)

Evri was called Hermes until 2022, and scammers use both names — our /guides/hermes-parcel-scam-text-uk/ guide covers the same scam under the old brand. They can set the sender name to read 'Evri' or 'Hermes', so a fake can sit beside genuine delivery texts, and the sender name proves nothing. They send the same message to huge lists of UK numbers, knowing most people are expecting a parcel. The fact that cuts through it: a genuine courier won't ask you to pay a fee or 'confirm' your address through a link in an unexpected text — you check and pay, if needed, through the courier's own app or website.


## 3. Signs an Evri text is a scam

- It asks you to pay a small fee — reschedule, 'release', or address confirmation — through a link.
- The link goes to a lookalike or unusual domain rather than the courier's real site; don't judge by the address alone — never pay or enter details through a text link.
- It pressures you with a deadline before the parcel is 'returned'.
- It asks for card details, bank details, or personal information.
- You weren't expecting a parcel, or the tracking number matches nothing you ordered.
- Errors can be clues — odd spacing, a foreign number — but a polished text can still be a scam.


## 4. How the Evri parcel scam works

First, a text claims a delivery failed or a small fee is due. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'reschedule' or 'pay'. Third, the link opens a fake Evri page that asks for your address and card details. Fourth, the criminals capture what you enter. Fifth, the tiny fee often goes through unnoticed — but they now have your card number, and many people then get a follow-up call from a fake 'bank' about the payment, which is the same gang. Finally, the details fuel larger fraud. Checking the parcel through the Evri app, never the text link, breaks the chain.


## 5. How to check an Evri text safely

Never tap the link or call a number from the text. Track the parcel directly: open the official Evri app or type evri.com into your browser yourself and enter your tracking number, or check the retailer you ordered from for the genuine tracking link. A real courier won't ask for a fee by text link; if a parcel truly can't be delivered, the Evri app or a card at your address will say so. When you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks.


## 6. If you paid or shared card details

Act quickly. If you entered card details or paid the 'fee', contact your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card, say it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment (a card payment may be recoverable through chargeback). Be wary of a follow-up call from a fake 'bank' about the payment — hang up and call your bank on the number on your card. If you sent a bank transfer, report it as fraud at once — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report an Evri scam text (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. You can also report fake delivery messages to Evri through their official website. Keep a screenshot and a note of what happened.


## FAQ

**Is Evri the same as Hermes?**

Yes — Hermes rebranded to Evri in 2022. It's a genuine courier, but scammers use both names in fake texts. Whether a text says 'Evri' or 'Hermes', treat it the same: don't tap the link; track your parcel in the official Evri app or on evri.com.

**Does Evri charge a redelivery fee by text?**

No. A genuine courier won't text you a link to pay a redelivery or 'release' fee. Any real arrangement is made through the courier's own app or website, never through a link in an unexpected text. A small 'fee' is the classic hook for stealing your card details.

**I paid the fee — can I get my money back?**

Possibly. Contact your bank or card issuer straight away using the number on your card, report it as a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment — card payments may be recoverable through chargeback. Watch for a follow-up 'bank' call about the payment; that's the same scammers.

**I clicked the link but didn't enter anything — am I at risk?**

Lower risk than entering details, but don't continue: close the page, enter nothing, and avoid any download it offers. If you did type card or personal details, treat them as compromised and call your bank. A device security scan is a sensible precaution.

**How do I report an Evri scam text?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider). If you lost money or shared details, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). You can also report fake delivery messages to Evri via their website.
