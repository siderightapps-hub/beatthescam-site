## 1. What a fake Santander email looks like

A Santander email scam is a phishing message that imitates the bank so you click a link and hand over your login details, card numbers, or personal information. A typical one arrives from a name like 'Santander Security' with a subject such as 'Your account has been temporarily locked', and a body that reads: 'Dear Customer, we detected unusual activity. Verify your identity within 24 hours to avoid suspension', above a 'Restore access' button. The pretext shifts — a locked account, a blocked payment, a 'new device' — but the goal is to rush you onto a fake Santander login page that captures whatever you enter. This guide shows how to tell a genuine Santander email from a fake, how to check safely, and what to do if you've already clicked.


## 2. Why these emails fool people

Phishing emails copy Santander's logo, colours and tone, and the visible sender name can be set to anything — so 'Santander Security' tells you nothing about who really sent it. The link text may read like santander.co.uk while pointing somewhere else entirely. Criminals send millions of these at once, knowing many recipients bank with Santander, and they lean on urgency and fear of losing access. But a genuine Santander email will never need you to follow a link to log in, confirm a password or passcode, or move money to a 'safe account' — and unlike a short text, a phishing email's generic 'Dear Customer' greeting and mismatched sender address are often a real giveaway.


## 3. Signs a Santander email is phishing

- A generic greeting like 'Dear Customer' instead of your name, paired with an urgent threat to your account.
- The sender's address isn't a genuine Santander address, or a link (hover over it without clicking) points to an unfamiliar domain — but addresses and links can be disguised, so never use an email link to log in, verify, or enter a code.
- It pushes you to 'verify', 'reactivate', or 'secure' your account through a button or link.
- It asks for your full password, PIN, card number, or a one-time passcode — Santander will never ask for these by email.
- It includes an unexpected attachment or tells you to move money to a 'safe' account.
- Spelling, spacing, or design that's slightly off, and a deadline to rush you — though many phishing emails are polished, so a tidy email proves nothing.


## 4. How the email scam works

First, an email lands that looks like a Santander security alert — your account is locked, or a payment needs confirming. Second, it pushes you to click a button to 'restore access' or 'verify'. Third, the link opens a fake Santander login page that mirrors the real site and asks for your details. Fourth, anything you type is captured by the criminals in real time. Fifth, they may trigger a genuine one-time passcode and ask you to enter it on the fake page, letting them log in or approve a payment. Finally, they take money or use your details for further fraud. Checking before you click — by going to Santander yourself, never via the email — stops the chain.


## 5. How to check if a Santander email is genuine

Don't click any link or open attachments. Instead, check independently: open the official Santander app, or type santander.co.uk into your browser by hand, and review your messages and recent activity there. You can hover over a link (without clicking) to preview where it really goes — but an address can still be disguised, so when in doubt, don't rely on it. To speak to the bank, call the number on the back of your card, or use 159 — a secure service, recognised across the UK banking industry, that connects you safely and directly to your bank, and Santander takes part. Not every genuine message appears in-app, so if you can't confirm what the email claims, contact Santander directly rather than assuming. ## 6. If you clicked or entered details

Act quickly but stay calm. If you entered your login or a passcode, call Santander on the number on your card, or use 159, tell them you've been phished, and ask them to secure your account and check for payments you didn't make. Change your Santander password from inside the official app or by typing the address yourself, and change it anywhere you reused it. If you shared card details, ask for the card to be stopped and reissued. If you sent money, report it as fraud at once — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement on UK transfers over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits, exclusions and a claiming deadline. If you opened an attachment, run a security scan. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion.


## 7. How to report a fake Santander email (UK)

Forward the email to the National Cyber Security Centre's Suspicious Email Reporting Service at report@phishing.gov.uk — it can investigate and take down phishing sites. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you're in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. Tell Santander too, through the app or the number on your card, so they can warn other customers. If the same scam also reached you by text, forward that to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider). Keep the email as evidence before deleting it.


## FAQ

**Does Santander send emails with links?**

Genuine bank emails can contain links, so a link alone isn't proof of a scam — but never use a link in an email to log in, confirm details, enter a one-time code, or move money. If an email asks for any of that, treat it as phishing and go to Santander yourself via the app or by typing santander.co.uk, not through the email.

**How can I tell who really sent a Santander email?**

The visible sender name can be faked, so check the actual email address — genuine Santander mail comes from a Santander domain — and hover over links (without clicking) to see where they really lead. A 'Dear Customer' greeting, a mismatched address, or a disguised link all point to phishing. When unsure, don't rely on the email at all; contact the bank independently.

**I clicked the link and entered my details — what now?**

Call Santander on the number on your card, or use 159, straight away and say you've been phished, so they can secure your account. Change your password in the official app, ask them to stop your card if you shared card details, and report any payment you didn't make. Then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk and consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk.

**Can I get my money back after a Santander email scam?**

Often, yes — report it to the bank immediately. Since 7 October 2024, most authorised push-payment fraud victims can claim reimbursement on UK transfers over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits, exclusions and a claiming deadline. If you paid by card, ask about a chargeback. Keep the email and every message as evidence.

**How do I report a fake Santander email?**

Forward it to the NCSC at report@phishing.gov.uk, then report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). Tell Santander too, through the app or the number on your card. If it also came as a text, forward that to 7726.
