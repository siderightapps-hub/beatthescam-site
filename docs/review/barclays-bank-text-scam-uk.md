## 1. What a fake Barclays text looks like

A Barclays scam text is a smishing message designed to look like a security alert so you tap a link and give up your login, card details, or a one-time passcode. A common one reads something like: 'Barclays: A new payee was set up on your account today. Not you? Cancel here: barclays-id-check.com'. The trigger varies — a new payee, a Direct Debit, a blocked card — but the goal is always to panic you into tapping. The link leads to a fake Barclays page that captures whatever you enter. This guide shows how to tell a genuine Barclays text from a fake, how to verify it safely, and what to do if you've already tapped one.


## 2. Why the fake is so convincing

Scammers can make the sender name show as 'Barclays', so a fake can sit in the same thread as your real messages — and a short code that looks like Barclays' can be spoofed too, so neither proves a text is genuine. They send identical messages to vast lists of UK numbers, knowing many bank with Barclays. The text copies real-alert wording, leans on urgency, and offers a single tap to 'fix' things. But a real Barclays alert will never need you to follow a link to log in, hand over a passcode, or move money to a 'safe account'.


## 3. Signs a Barclays text is a scam

- It pressures you — a payee 'added', a payment 'blocked', access lost unless you act fast.
- It links you out to 'cancel', 'verify' or 'secure' your account, log in, or re-enter card details.
- The link goes to a lookalike or unusual domain. Banks may use more than one genuine owned domain, so do not judge by the address alone — the rule that always holds is never to use a text-message link to log in, verify details, enter a code, or move money. Open the Barclays app or type the address yourself.
- It asks for your full passcode, PIN, card number, or a one-time passcode — Barclays will never ask you to share these.
- It wants you to move money to a new or 'safe' account.
- A 'Dear Customer' greeting is a weak sign by text (real bank texts are often short) — focus on what it asks you to do.
- Small errors can be clues — odd spacing, a foreign number, or wording that isn't quite official — but many scam texts are polished, so a tidy message proves nothing.


## 4. How the scam works, step by step

First, a text appears to be a Barclays alert about a new payee, a Direct Debit, or a blocked payment. Second, it urges you to tap a link to 'stop' it or 'confirm it's you'. Third, the link opens a fake Barclays page asking for your membership number, passcode and memorable word. Fourth, the moment you enter them, the criminals have them in real time. Fifth, they prompt a genuine one-time passcode and the page asks you to type it in, letting them log in or approve a payment. Finally, they move money or add payees of their own. Because it can take only minutes, pausing at the first text is what protects you.


## 5. How to check a Barclays text safely

Never use a number or link from the text itself. The simplest check is to call 159 — a secure service, recognised across the UK banking industry, that connects you safely and directly to your bank, and Barclays takes part. You can also open the official Barclays app, or type barclays.co.uk into your browser by hand, and review your recent activity and messages there. Or call Barclays on the number on the back of your card. Not every genuine text shows up as an in-app message, so if you can't confirm what the text claims, contact Barclays directly rather than assuming. If you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks.


## 6. If you tapped the link or shared details

Act quickly but stay calm. If you entered details or a passcode, call 159 or the number on your card straight away, tell Barclays you've been phished, and ask them to secure the account and check for payees or payments you didn't make. Change your Barclays passcode in the official app or by typing the address yourself, and anywhere you reused it. If you shared card details, ask for the card to be stopped and reissued. If you sent money, report it as fraud immediately — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement on UK transfers over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits, exclusions and a claiming deadline. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report a Barclays scam text (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you're in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. Tell Barclays through the app or the number on your card, and keep a screenshot and a note of what you did and when.


## FAQ

**Is a Barclays text with a link always a scam?**

A link alone isn't proof either way. What counts is what it asks: never use a link in a text to log in, confirm details, enter a one-time code, or move money. If a message pushes any of that, treat it as a scam and check through the Barclays app, the number on your card, or 159.

**I clicked the link but didn't enter anything — am I at risk?**

Tapping a link alone is lower-risk than entering details, but don't assume you're safe: don't type anything on the page, close it, and avoid downloading any file it offers. If you entered any detail or a passcode, treat it as compromised and call Barclays on 159 or the number on your card. Running a security scan on your device is a sensible precaution.

**Can a fake Barclays text appear in my real alerts thread?**

Yes. Scammers can spoof the sender name so 'Barclays' shows, and even mimic a short code, which can place a fake among genuine alerts. Never trust a text by its sender name or position — verify independently before you act.

**What number should I call to check safely?**

Call 159 — a secure service that connects you safely and directly to your bank, and Barclays takes part — or use the number on the back of your card. Never call a number given in the suspicious text.

**How do I report a Barclays text scam?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider), then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). If it came by email, send it to report@phishing.gov.uk. Tell Barclays too, through the app or the number on your card.
