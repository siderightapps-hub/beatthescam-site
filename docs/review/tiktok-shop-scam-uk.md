## 1. What a TikTok Shop scam looks like

Most TikTok Shop scams aim to get your money outside TikTok's checkout, where there's no protection — or to sell you counterfeit or non-existent goods. A seller might push you to pay by bank transfer or PayPal 'friends and family' for a 'discount', a viral listing might offer a branded item at an impossible price, or a direct message might claim `You've won our TikTok giveaway! Claim your prize here: tiktok-prize.example`. The safe thread through all of it: buy and pay only through TikTok Shop's own checkout. This guide shows the common cons, how to check, and how to report.


## 2. Why TikTok Shop scams catch people out

TikTok's huge reach and fast, video-driven shopping make impulse buys easy, and scammers ride viral trends with deals that feel time-limited. They copy real brands and stock photos, and they move buyers into direct messages where there's less oversight. The biggest trap is leaving TikTok Shop: its buyer protections only apply when you pay through its checkout, so the moment a seller asks for a bank transfer, gift card, or PayPal 'friends and family', that protection is gone — and a genuine seller has no reason to take payment off-platform.


## 3. Signs of a TikTok Shop scam

- A seller asks you to pay outside TikTok Shop's checkout — by bank transfer, gift card, or PayPal 'friends and family'.
- A branded item is priced far below the normal retail price.
- You're pushed to buy now before a 'limited' deal ends.
- A direct message says you've won a giveaway and links you off TikTok to 'claim'.
- The seller has no ratings, few real reviews, or only stock photos.
- You're asked for card or bank details outside the normal checkout.
- Errors can be clues, but a polished shopfront can still be a scam.


## 4. How the TikTok Shop scam works

First, a viral listing, live stream, or direct message offers a great deal or a prize. Second, it moves you off TikTok's checkout — into DMs, a bank transfer, or an external link. Third, you pay by an untraceable method, or enter card details on a fake page. Fourth, the item never arrives, is a cheap counterfeit, or the 'prize' is a way to harvest your details. Finally, because the payment left TikTok Shop, its buyer protection doesn't apply. Keeping the whole purchase inside TikTok Shop is what protects you.


## 5. How to check and buy safely on TikTok Shop

Keep the purchase inside TikTok Shop.
- Pay only through TikTok Shop's checkout — never by bank transfer, gift card, or PayPal 'friends and family'.
- Treat a branded item at an impossible price as counterfeit or a non-delivery scam.
- Check the seller's ratings and genuine reviews before buying.
- Ignore 'you've won' direct messages that link you off TikTok to claim a prize.
- Never enter card or bank details on a page reached from a DM or external link.
If you're unsure whether a linked site is genuine, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/is-temu-a-scam-uk/ guide covers similar cheap-marketplace risks.


## 6. If you've paid or shared details

Act quickly. If you paid by card or through PayPal 'goods and services', open a dispute and ask your card issuer about a chargeback (note that PayPal 'friends and family' payments aren't covered). If you sent a bank transfer, report it as fraud immediately — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. If you entered card or bank details on a fake page, contact your bank using the number on your card. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the listing, messages, and receipts as evidence.


## 7. How to report a TikTok Shop scam (UK)

Report the seller, product, or message inside TikTok using its report options, so the platform can act. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk; if by text, forward it to 7726. Keep screenshots of everything.


## FAQ

**Is TikTok Shop a scam, or just some sellers?**

TikTok Shop is a genuine marketplace, but some sellers on it are scammers, and others sell counterfeit or poor-quality goods. The platform being real doesn't make every seller or deal genuine — so check ratings, be wary of impossible prices, and always pay through TikTok Shop's checkout.

**A seller wants PayPal 'friends and family' instead of normal checkout — is that safe?**

No. Paying by PayPal 'friends and family', bank transfer, or gift card removes your protection, which is exactly why scammers ask for it. Keep the payment in TikTok Shop's own checkout; a genuine seller has no reason to take it off-platform.

**I got a TikTok message saying I won a giveaway — is it real?**

Treat it as a scam. 'You've won, claim your prize here' messages that link you off TikTok are designed to harvest your details or payment. Don't tap the link; report and ignore it.

**I've already paid a TikTok Shop scammer — can I get my money back?**

Possibly. If you paid by card or PayPal 'goods and services', open a dispute or chargeback. If you sent a bank transfer, report it as fraud at once: since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Report the seller to TikTok and keep all the messages.

**How do I report a TikTok Shop scam?**

Report the seller or listing inside TikTok, then report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101).
