## 1. What a fake NatWest email looks like

A NatWest phishing email imitates the bank so you click a link and hand over your login, card details, or a one-time code. A typical one comes from a display name like `NatWest` with a subject such as `Unusual activity detected on your account` or `Action required: verify your details`, and a button like `Restore access`. The pretext changes — a blocked payment, a new device, a locked account — but the goal is to rush you onto a fake NatWest sign-in page. The link often points to a lookalike such as `natwest-secure-verify.example`. This guide shows how to tell a genuine NatWest email from phishing, how to check safely, and how to report it.


## 2. Why these NatWest emails fool people

Phishing emails copy NatWest's branding, and the visible sender name can be set to anything, so 'NatWest' in the From line proves nothing. The link text may read like natwest.com while pointing elsewhere, and the message may even come from a hacked mailbox. Criminals send millions at once, knowing many recipients bank with NatWest, and lean on urgency — unusual activity, a locked account, a deadline. None of that makes it real: a genuine NatWest email won't ask you to confirm your full password, PIN, card number, or a one-time passcode by following a link, or to move money to a 'safe account'.


## 3. Signs a NatWest email is phishing

- A generic greeting such as `Dear Customer` instead of your name, paired with an urgent warning.
- The sender's address isn't a genuine NatWest address, or a link (hover without clicking) points to an unfamiliar site — but these can be disguised, so never sign in from an email link.
- It pushes you to `verify`, `restore`, or `secure` your account through a button or link.
- It asks for your password, PIN, full card number, or a one-time passcode.
- It tells you to move money to a new or 'safe' account.
- An unexpected attachment, or spelling and layout that's slightly off — though polished emails can still be phishing.


## 4. How the NatWest email scam works

First, an email lands that looks like a NatWest security alert. Second, it pushes you to click a button to 'verify' or 'restore' access. Third, the link opens a near-perfect copy of the NatWest sign-in page. Fourth, anything you type — customer number, password, card details — is captured in real time. Fifth, some pages then ask for a one-time passcode so the criminals can get past two-step verification. Finally, they access the account, move money, or use the details elsewhere. Checking through the NatWest app or by typing natwest.com yourself, not the email, stops it.


## 5. How to check if a NatWest email is genuine

Don't click links or open attachments in the email.
- Open the official NatWest app, or type natwest.com into your browser yourself, and check your messages and recent activity.
- Hover over a link (without clicking) to preview where it really goes — but don't rely on that alone, as links can be disguised.
- To speak to the bank, call the number on the back of your card, or use 159, a secure service that connects you safely to your bank, which NatWest takes part in.
- If you can't confirm what the email claims, contact NatWest directly rather than assuming.
Our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ helps you check a suspicious linked site, and our /guides/natwest-fraud-text-message-scam-uk/ guide covers the same scam by text.


## 6. If you clicked or shared details

Act quickly. If you entered your login or a passcode, call NatWest on 159 or the number on your card, tell them you've been phished, and ask them to secure the account and check for payments or new payees you didn't set up. Change your NatWest password from inside the official app or by typing the address yourself, and change it anywhere you reused it. If you shared card details, ask for the card to be stopped and reissued. If you sent money, report it as fraud at once — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the email as evidence.


## 7. How to report a fake NatWest email (UK)

Forward the email to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk, which can investigate and take down phishing sites. Report it to NatWest too, through their official website, so they can warn other customers. If you lost money or shared sensitive details, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If the same scam reached you by text, forward it to 7726.


## FAQ

**Does NatWest send emails with links?**

Genuine NatWest emails can contain links, so a link alone isn't proof of a scam — but never use an email link to sign in, confirm your password or card details, or enter a one-time code. If an email asks for any of that, treat it as phishing and go to NatWest yourself via the app or by typing natwest.com.

**How can I tell who really sent a NatWest email?**

The visible sender name can be faked, so check the full email address and hover over links (without clicking) to see where they lead. The most reliable test is your account itself: sign in via the app or natwest.com directly and check whether the alert appears in your genuine messages.

**I clicked the link and entered my details — what now?**

Call NatWest on 159 or the number on your card straight away and say you've been phished, so they can secure your account. Change your password in the official app, ask them to stop your card if you shared card details, and report any payment you didn't make. Then forward the email to report@phishing.gov.uk.

**Can I get my money back after a NatWest email scam?**

Often, yes. Report it to the bank immediately. If you sent a UK bank transfer, since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. If you paid by card, ask about a chargeback. Keep the email and every message as evidence.

**How do I report a fake NatWest email?**

Forward it to the NCSC at report@phishing.gov.uk and report it to NatWest through their website. If you lost money, also report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101).
