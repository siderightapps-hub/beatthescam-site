## 1. What a fake NHS text looks like

An NHS scam text is a smishing message that pretends to come from the NHS so you tap a link and hand over personal, bank, or card details. A common one reads: `NHS: your upcoming appointment has been cancelled. To rebook, confirm your details here: nhs-rebook.example`, while others claim you're 'eligible' for a payment, a grant, or treatment if you 'verify' now. The NHS does send genuine appointment reminders, which is what makes the fakes effective — but NHS care is free, and the NHS won't ask you to pay or hand over bank details through a text link. This guide shows how to tell a real NHS text from a scam, how to check safely, and how to report it.


## 2. Why a fake NHS text is convincing

Scammers spoof the sender so a message shows as 'NHS', and they send the same text to huge numbers of people, knowing almost everyone is registered with the NHS. They use the worry of a lost appointment or the lure of a payment or 'free' treatment. Two facts cut through it: NHS treatment is free at the point of use, so any text asking you to pay for an appointment, a vaccine, or a 'grant' is a scam; and while the NHS does text genuine reminders, it won't ask you to confirm bank or card details, or make a payment, by following a link in a text. Check through the NHS App or your GP surgery, not the message.


## 3. Signs an NHS text is a scam

- It asks you to pay for an appointment, treatment, a vaccine, or a test — NHS care is free at the point of use.
- It offers a 'payment', grant, or refund you must claim through a link.
- It asks for bank details, card details, or your full personal information to 'confirm' or 'verify'.
- The link isn't an nhs.uk address — it's a lookalike or a shortened link.
- It pressures you to act immediately or lose your place or appointment.
- It can't be matched to a real appointment when you check the NHS App or with your GP.
- Errors can be clues, but a tidy, official-looking text can still be a scam.


## 4. How the NHS text scam works

First, a text claims your appointment has changed, or that you're eligible for a payment or treatment. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'rebook', 'confirm', or 'claim'. Third, the link opens a fake NHS-style page that asks for your personal details and, often, bank or card information to 'verify your identity' or take a small 'fee'. Fourth, the criminals capture whatever you enter. Fifth, they use it to take payments, commit identity fraud, or target you again. Because a health message feels important and urgent, people respond quickly — so checking only through the NHS App or your GP breaks the chain.


## 5. How to check an NHS text safely

Don't tap the link or call a number from the text.
- Check the NHS App or your NHS account, where genuine appointments and messages appear.
- Contact your GP surgery or the hospital directly, using a number you find yourself — on a letter, your records, or the official nhs.uk website.
- Remember NHS care is free: a request to pay, or for bank or card details, is a scam.
- If a text can't be matched to a real appointment, treat it as fake.
If you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/dvla-vehicle-tax-text-scam/ guide covers the same trick using other official names.


## 6. If you clicked or shared details

Act quickly. If you entered card details or made a payment, contact your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card, tell them it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment (a card payment may be recoverable through chargeback). If you made a bank transfer, report it as fraud immediately — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. If you shared personal information, consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report an NHS scam text (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. If a message misuses the NHS name, you can also report it through the official nhs.uk website. Keep a screenshot of the message.


## FAQ

**Does the NHS send appointment texts?**

Yes — the NHS does send genuine appointment reminders and updates by text, so a text isn't automatically a scam. But a real NHS text won't ask you to pay, hand over bank or card details, or 'confirm' personal information through a link. Check anything about an appointment in the NHS App or with your GP surgery directly.

**The NHS is free — so why would an NHS text ask for payment?**

It wouldn't — that's how you know it's a scam. NHS care is free at the point of use, so any text asking you to pay for an appointment, vaccine, test, or to 'release' a payment to you is fraudulent. Don't tap the link or enter any details.

**Can the NHS really cancel my appointment by text?**

Genuine reminders and changes can come by text, but you should never act on a link in one. If a text says your appointment is cancelled or changed, check it in the NHS App or by contacting your GP surgery or hospital directly — not through the message.

**I clicked the link and entered my details — what now?**

If you entered card or bank details, call your bank using the number on your card and ask them to stop the card and watch the account. If you shared personal information, consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk. Then report the scam text to 7726 and to Report Fraud.

**How do I report an NHS scam text?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider), then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). You can also report misuse of the NHS name via nhs.uk.
