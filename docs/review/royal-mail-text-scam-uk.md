## 1. What a fake Royal Mail text looks like

A Royal Mail scam text is a smishing message that pretends to be from Royal Mail so you tap a link and hand over card or personal details. A typical one reads something like: `Royal Mail: your parcel has an unpaid shipping fee of £2.99. Pay to reschedule delivery: royalmail-redelivery.example`. The fee is always small and believable, because a low amount lowers your guard while the real target is your card number. The link opens a fake page that captures whatever you type. This guide shows how to tell a fake from a genuine Royal Mail message, how to pay any real fee safely, and how to report the scam.


## 2. Why Royal Mail texts are easy to fake

Scammers can set the sender name to read 'Royal Mail', so a fake can appear beside genuine delivery texts — the sender name proves nothing. They send the same message to millions of UK numbers, knowing most people are expecting post. There is a genuine wrinkle the scammers exploit: Royal Mail does sometimes charge a real fee, for example a customs charge or a surcharge on underpaid postage. But a genuine charge comes with a grey `Fee to Pay` card through your door, or is handled on royalmail.com — it is never collected through a link in an unexpected text that asks for your card details.


## 3. Signs a Royal Mail text is a scam

- It asks you to pay a small fee — redelivery, customs, or a postage 'shortfall' — through a link.
- The link isn't royalmail.com; it's a lookalike or shortened address. Don't judge by the address alone — never pay through a link in a text.
- It pressures you with a deadline before the item is 'returned to sender'.
- It asks for card details, bank details, or personal information.
- You weren't expecting anything that would attract a fee, or the message can't tell you what the item is.
- Errors can be clues — odd spacing, a foreign number — but a polished, tidy text can still be a scam.


## 4. How the Royal Mail fee scam works

First, a text claims your parcel needs a small fee paid before it can be delivered. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'pay' and 'reschedule'. Third, the link opens a fake Royal Mail page asking for your address and card details. Fourth, the criminals capture them. Fifth, the small fee often goes through without you noticing — but they now hold your card number, and many victims then get a call from a fake 'bank fraud team' about the payment, which is the same gang going after the rest. Checking on royalmail.com instead of the link stops it at the first step.


## 5. How to check a Royal Mail text safely

Never tap the link or call a number from the text. To check a delivery or pay a genuine fee, type royalmail.com into your browser yourself (or use the official Royal Mail app) and use Track & Trace, or follow the grey `Fee to Pay` card left at your address. If there's a real customs or postage charge, royalmail.com is where you settle it — not a texted link. If the message can't be matched to a real item in tracking, treat it as a scam. When you're not sure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/dpd-delivery-scam-text/ guide covers the same trick from other couriers.


## 6. If you paid or shared card details

Act quickly. If you entered card details or paid the 'fee', contact your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card, say it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment (a card payment may be recoverable through chargeback). Be ready for a follow-up call from a fake 'bank' about the payment — hang up and call your bank on the number on your card. If you made a bank transfer, report it as fraud at once — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report a Royal Mail scam text (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam arrived by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. You can also report fake delivery messages to Royal Mail through their official website. Keep a screenshot and a note of what happened.


## FAQ

**Does Royal Mail charge a fee by text?**

Not by a link in a text. Royal Mail does sometimes charge a genuine fee, such as a customs charge or a postage shortfall — but you'll get a grey `Fee to Pay` card through your door, or you settle it on royalmail.com. A text with a link asking for a small fee and your card details is a scam.

**How do I tell a genuine Royal Mail fee from a scam?**

Check it independently on royalmail.com (or the official app) using your tracking number, or wait for the grey `Fee to Pay` card. Genuine charges are never collected through a link in an unexpected text. If you can't match the message to a real item, it's a scam.

**I paid the fee — can I get my money back?**

Possibly. Contact your bank or card issuer immediately using the number on your card, report it as a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment; card payments may be recoverable through chargeback. Watch for a follow-up 'bank' call about the payment — that's the scammers again.

**I clicked the link but didn't pay — am I at risk?**

Lower risk than entering details, but don't continue: close the page, enter nothing, and don't download anything it offers. If you did enter card or personal details, treat them as compromised and call your bank. A device security scan is a sensible precaution.

**How do I report a Royal Mail text scam?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider). If you lost money or shared details, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). You can also report it to Royal Mail via their website.
