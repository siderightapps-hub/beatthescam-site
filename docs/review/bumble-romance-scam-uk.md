## 1. What a Bumble scam looks like

A Bumble scam is a romance scam: someone uses a fake or misleading profile to build trust, then exploits it for money, details, or intimate images. The pattern is consistent across dating apps — a match who seems ideal pushes the chat off Bumble to WhatsApp or Telegram, talks about strong feelings within days, and always has a reason they can't meet or video-call. Then comes the ask: a sudden emergency (a medical bill, being stranded abroad, a customs fee), or a 'great' crypto or investment opportunity. Anyone can be targeted — these criminals are patient and practised. This guide shows the signs, how to protect yourself, and how to report it; our /guides/tinder-scam-uk/ guide covers the same pattern on other apps.


## 2. Why romance scams are so convincing

Romance scammers invest time — sometimes weeks — building trust before they ask for anything, which is what makes it hard to spot. They use attractive stolen photos, claim jobs that explain why they're far away or can't meet (working overseas, in the military, on an oil rig, a doctor abroad), and mirror your interests. The connection feels real to you even though the person isn't who they claim, and by the time money is mentioned, refusing can feel like letting someone down. Recognising the pattern early — not blaming yourself — is the protection.


## 3. Signs a Bumble match is a scammer

- They push to move off Bumble to WhatsApp, Telegram, or another private app very quickly.
- They profess strong feelings unusually fast, before you've met.
- They always have a reason they can't meet in person or do a live video call.
- Their story explains the distance — overseas work, military deployment, an oil rig, a contract abroad.
- They eventually ask for money — an emergency, travel, customs, or medical costs — usually by transfer, gift card, or cryptocurrency.
- Or they steer you toward a crypto or investment 'opportunity' with guaranteed returns (a 'pig-butchering' scam).
- They ask for intimate photos, which can later be used to threaten you.
- Their details don't add up, or their photos appear elsewhere online under other names.


## 4. How the romance scam works

First, a fake profile matches with you and moves the chat to a private app. Second, over days or weeks they build a strong bond and avoid meeting or video-calling. Third, a crisis appears, or an investment 'opportunity' is dangled. Fourth, they ask for money — often by transfer, gift card, or crypto, which is hard to trace and recover. Fifth, once you pay, the requests usually continue with new reasons until you stop. In pig-butchering scams, a fake investment site may show invented profits to draw in more money, then demand 'fees' or 'taxes' when you try to withdraw. The safest point to act is before any money or crypto changes hands.


## 5. How to protect yourself

Keep your guard up until you've genuinely verified who you're talking to.
- Keep chatting within Bumble until you've met in person or had a live video call you're sure is real.
- Do a reverse-image search of their photos to see if they appear under other names.
- Never send money, gift cards, or cryptocurrency to someone you haven't met in person — whatever the story.
- Be very wary of any investment or crypto 'tip' from a romantic interest; guaranteed returns are a scam.
- Insist on a live video call, and treat repeated excuses as a serious warning sign.
If a link or 'investment platform' they sent looks doubtful, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/military-romance-scam-uk/ guide covers a common cover story in detail.


## 6. If you've sent money or shared details

First, this is not your fault — these criminals are skilled, and the embarrassment they rely on is exactly what stops people reporting. If you sent money by UK bank transfer, contact your bank immediately using the number on your card and report it as fraud; since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Money sent by cryptocurrency or gift card is much harder to recover, so report it quickly and stop any further payments. Block and report the profile, and keep the messages. If you shared personal details, consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk. If you shared intimate images and are being threatened, do not pay — that is sextortion; our /guides/sextortion-email-scam-uk/ guide explains what to do.


## 7. How to report a Bumble scam (UK)

Report and block the profile inside Bumble (use the block/report option on their profile or chat), so the platform can review the account. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. For free, confidential advice, contact the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133. Keep screenshots of the profile and your conversations before blocking.


## FAQ

**Is Bumble safe, or is everyone on it a scammer?**

Bumble is a genuine dating app and most people use it honestly — but romance scammers operate on every dating platform. The app being real doesn't make every match genuine, so watch for the pattern: moving off the app fast, avoiding video calls, and eventually asking for money or pushing an investment.

**My Bumble match needs money for an emergency — should I send it?**

No. A request for money — for an emergency, travel, customs, or medical costs — from someone you haven't met in person is the core of a romance scam, however urgent it sounds. Don't send money, gift cards, or crypto. A genuine partner you've never met won't ask.

**My match suggested a crypto or investment opportunity — is that a scam?**

Almost certainly. A romantic interest steering you toward crypto or an 'investment platform' with guaranteed returns is a well-known scam, often called 'pig butchering'. The platform is fake and any 'profits' shown are bait. Don't invest, and stop sending money.

**I've already sent money — can I get it back?**

Possibly. Tell your bank immediately. For UK bank transfers, since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Money sent by cryptocurrency or gift card is much harder to recover, so report it quickly and keep all the messages.

**How do I report a Bumble romance scam?**

Report and block the profile in Bumble first so it can be reviewed, then report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). If you sent money, tell your bank straight away.
