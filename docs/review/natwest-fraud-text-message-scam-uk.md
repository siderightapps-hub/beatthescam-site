## 1. The fake NatWest text — and what it's really after

A NatWest fraud-text scam is a smishing message built to look like a genuine security alert so you tap a link and surrender your login, card details, or a one-time passcode. A typical one reads something like: 'NatWest: We blocked a £420 payment from a new device. If this wasn't you, confirm here: natwest-secure-login.com'. The story varies — a blocked payment, a new device, a frozen card — but the aim is to frighten you into tapping before you think. The link opens a fake NatWest login page that captures everything you type. Below: how to tell a genuine NatWest text from a fake, how to verify it safely, and what to do if you've already responded.


## 2. Why it slips past your guard

Scammers can set the sender name to display as 'NatWest', so a fake can appear in the very same thread as real alerts — which is why the sender name and a familiar-looking short code prove nothing (criminals spoof both). They blast the same message to long lists of UK numbers, confident some recipients bank with NatWest. The text mimics a real fraud alert's tone, adds urgency, and offers a one-tap 'fix'. But a genuine NatWest alert will never need you to follow a link to log in, share a passcode, or move money to a 'safe account' — those asks are the scam.


## 3. Red flags in a NatWest scam text

- It manufactures urgency — a payment 'blocked', a device 'added', access lost unless you act now.
- It sends you to a link to 'confirm', 'restore', or 'secure' your account, log in, or re-enter card details.
- The link goes to a lookalike or unusual domain. Banks may use more than one genuine owned domain, so do not judge by the address alone — the rule that always holds is never to use a text-message link to log in, verify details, enter a code, or move money. Open the NatWest app or type the address yourself.
- It asks for your PIN, full password, card number, or a one-time passcode — NatWest will never ask you to share these.
- It tells you to move money to a new or 'safe' account.
- A 'Dear Customer' greeting is a weak clue by text (genuine bank texts are often short) — judge it by what it asks, not how it opens.
- Small errors can be clues — odd spacing, a foreign number, or wording that isn't quite official — but many scam texts are polished, so a tidy message proves nothing.


## 4. Step by step: how the scam works

First, a text arrives appearing to be a NatWest fraud alert — a payment stopped, or a login from an unknown device. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'confirm it's you' or 'restore access'. Third, the link opens a near-perfect fake NatWest page asking for your customer number, PIN digits and password. Fourth, as you type, the criminals capture them live. Fifth, they trigger a genuine one-time passcode and the page asks you to enter it, letting them log in or approve a payment. Finally, they drain the account or set up new payees. The whole thing can take minutes, so the safest move is to stop at the first text and check independently.


## 5. How to check a NatWest text safely

Never use a number or link from the text. The simplest check is to call 159 — a secure service, recognised across the UK banking industry, that connects you safely and directly to your bank, and NatWest takes part. You can also open the official NatWest app, or type natwest.com into your browser by hand, and review recent activity and messages there. Or call NatWest on the number on the back of your card. Not every genuine text appears as an in-app message, so if you can't confirm what the text claims, contact NatWest directly rather than assuming either way. Unsure whether a linked site is a copycat? Our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ runs through the checks.


## 6. Already responded? Do this now

Move fast, but stay calm. If you entered details or a passcode, call 159 or the number on your card immediately, tell NatWest you've been phished, and ask them to secure the account and check for payments or payees you didn't set up. Change your NatWest password in the official app or by typing the address yourself, and anywhere you reused it. If you shared card details, ask for the card to be stopped and reissued. If you sent money, report it as fraud at once — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement on UK transfers over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits, exclusions and a claiming deadline. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. Where to report a NatWest scam text

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you're in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam came by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. Tell NatWest through the app or the number on your card. Keep a screenshot and a note of what you did and when.


## FAQ

**Is a NatWest text with a link always a scam?**

Not on its own — a link alone isn't proof either way. The test is what it asks you to do: never use a link in a text to log in, confirm details, enter a one-time code, or move money. If it pushes any of those, treat it as a scam and check through the NatWest app, the number on your card, or 159.

**Can a fake NatWest text land in my genuine alerts thread?**

Yes — scammers can spoof the sender name so 'NatWest' shows, placing a fake among your real alerts, and a familiar-looking short code can be faked too. Never trust a text by its sender name or position; verify it independently first.

**What's the safest number to call to check?**

Call 159 — a secure service that connects you safely and directly to your bank, and NatWest takes part — or use the number printed on the back of your card. Never call a number supplied in the suspicious text.

**Can I get my money back after a NatWest text scam?**

Often, yes. Report it to your bank immediately. Since 7 October 2024, most authorised push-payment fraud victims can claim reimbursement on UK transfers over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits, exclusions and a claiming deadline. If you paid by card, ask about a chargeback. Keep every message as evidence.

**How do I report a NatWest fraud text?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider), then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). If it arrived by email, send it to report@phishing.gov.uk. Tell NatWest too, via the app or the number on your card.
