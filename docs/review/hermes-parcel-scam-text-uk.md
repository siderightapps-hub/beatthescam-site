## 1. What a fake Hermes text looks like

A Hermes parcel scam text is a smishing message that pretends to be from the courier so you tap a link and hand over card or personal details. A typical one reads something like: `Hermes: we couldn't deliver your parcel. Pay the £1.45 redelivery fee here: hermes-redelivery.example`. The hook is usually a small, believable fee — a 'redelivery', a 'customs charge', an 'address confirmation' — because a low amount lowers your guard while the real prize is your card details. The link opens a fake page that captures whatever you enter. This guide shows how to spot the fake, check your parcel safely, and report it.


## 2. Hermes is now Evri — and why the scam still works

Hermes rebranded to Evri in 2022, but scam texts still use the old 'Hermes' name because people recognise it, and our /guides/evri-delivery-scam-guide/ guide covers the same scam under the new brand. Scammers can set the sender name to read 'Hermes' or 'Evri', so a fake can sit alongside genuine delivery texts — the sender name proves nothing. They blast the same message to huge lists of UK numbers, knowing most people are expecting a parcel. The key fact cuts through it: a genuine courier will not ask you to pay a fee or 'confirm' your address through a link in an unexpected text, and you should always check using the courier's own app or website.


## 3. Signs a Hermes (Evri) parcel text is a scam

- It asks you to pay a small fee — redelivery, customs, or 'release' — through a link.
- The link goes to a lookalike or unusual domain, not the courier's real site; don't judge by the address alone — never pay or enter details through a link in a text.
- It pressures you with a short deadline before the parcel is 'returned'.
- It asks for card details, bank details, or personal information.
- You weren't expecting a parcel, or the tracking number doesn't match anything you ordered.
- Small errors can be clues — odd spacing, a foreign number — but a polished text can still be a scam.


## 4. How the parcel-fee scam works

First, a text claims a delivery failed or a small fee is due. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'pay' or 'reschedule'. Third, the link opens a fake courier page that asks for your address and card details. Fourth, the criminals capture what you enter. Fifth, because the fee is tiny, the card payment often goes through unnoticed — but they now have your card number, and some pages follow up pretending to be your bank to chase the rest. Finally, they use the details for larger fraud. Checking the parcel through the courier's own app, never the text link, stops it.


## 5. How to check a parcel text safely

Never tap the link or call a number from the text. Instead, check the parcel directly: open the official Evri app or type evri.com into your browser yourself (Hermes deliveries are now Evri) and enter your tracking number, or check the retailer you ordered from for the real tracking link. A genuine courier won't ask for a fee by text link; any real charge is handled through the courier's own site. If a parcel really can't be delivered, the courier's app or a card at your address will tell you. When you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks.


## 6. If you paid or shared card details

Act quickly. If you entered card details or paid the 'fee', contact your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card, tell them it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment (a card payment may be recoverable through chargeback). Watch for a follow-up call pretending to be your bank about the 'suspicious payment' — that's the same gang; hang up and call your bank on the number on your card. If you sent a bank transfer, report it as fraud immediately — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report a Hermes (Evri) scam text (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. You can also report fake delivery messages to Evri through their official website so they can warn others. Keep a screenshot and a note of what happened.


## FAQ

**Is Hermes still going, or is it Evri now?**

Hermes rebranded to Evri in 2022, so the company is genuine and still delivering — but scammers keep using the better-known 'Hermes' name in fake texts. Treat a 'Hermes' or 'Evri' text the same way: don't tap the link; check your parcel in the official Evri app or on evri.com.

**Does Hermes or Evri charge a redelivery fee by text?**

No. A genuine courier won't text you a link to pay a redelivery, customs, or 'release' fee. Any real charge is arranged through the courier's own website or app, never through a link in an unexpected text. A small 'fee' is the classic hook for capturing your card details.

**I paid the 'fee' — can I get my money back?**

Possibly. Contact your bank or card issuer straight away using the number on your card, report it as a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment — card payments may be recoverable through chargeback. Watch for a follow-up 'bank' call about the payment; that's the same scammers.

**I tapped the link but didn't pay — am I at risk?**

Tapping alone is lower-risk than entering details, but don't continue: close the page, don't enter anything, and don't download any file it offers. If you did type card or personal details, treat them as compromised and call your bank. Running a security scan on your device is a sensible precaution.

**How do I report a Hermes scam text?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider). If you lost money or shared details, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). You can also report fake delivery messages to Evri via their website.
