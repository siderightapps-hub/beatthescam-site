## 1. What eBay scams look like

Most eBay scams share one goal: to move your money or your details off eBay, where there's no protection. As a seller, a fake 'buyer' may send a screenshot of a payment that never arrives, or 'overpay' and ask for the difference back. As a buyer, a seller may push you to pay by bank transfer outside eBay, or send an email like `eBay: your payment is held by Buyer Protection — confirm your bank details to release it: ebay-secure-pay.example`. The safe thread through all of it: keep messages and payment on eBay, and never pay or get paid off-platform. This guide shows the common cons, how to check, and how to report.


## 2. Why eBay scams catch people out

Scam emails and messages copy eBay's branding, and a sense of urgency — a payment 'held', an item about to be relisted, a buyer 'ready to pay more' — pushes people to act fast. The biggest trap is going off-platform: eBay's Money Back Guarantee and seller protections only apply when you buy and pay through eBay, so the moment a deal moves to bank transfer, gift cards, or WhatsApp, that protection is gone. eBay will never email you asking for your bank details to 'release' a payment, and a genuine buyer or seller has no reason to take the deal off eBay.


## 3. Signs of an eBay scam

- A buyer or seller asks to pay, get paid, or message outside eBay — by bank transfer, gift card, PayPal 'friends and family', or WhatsApp.
- A 'buyer' overpays and asks you to refund the difference, or sends a payment screenshot you can't see in your own account.
- An 'eBay' email asks for your bank details, password, or card number to 'release a held payment' or 'verify your account'.
- The link in an email isn't eBay's real site; it's a lookalike or shortened address.
- Pressure to post an item before the payment shows in your account, or to buy at a too-good price away from eBay.
- Errors can be clues, but a polished message can still be a scam.


## 4. How the eBay scam works

First, a buyer, seller, or fake 'eBay' message gives you a reason to act — a payment to release, an overpayment to refund, or a cheaper off-platform deal. Second, it moves you off eBay or onto a fake page. Third, you pay by an untraceable method, post goods before any real payment, or enter your bank details. Fourth, the payment turns out to be fake or never existed. Finally, because the deal left eBay, the Money Back Guarantee and seller protection don't apply, and the money or goods are gone. Keeping everything on eBay is what protects you.


## 5. How to check and stay protected on eBay

Keep the whole transaction on eBay.
- Pay and get paid only through eBay's checkout; never by bank transfer, gift card, or 'friends and family'.
- Check messages and orders in the eBay app or by typing ebay.co.uk yourself — not through a link in an email.
- For sellers: confirm a payment has actually arrived in your account before posting, and ignore 'overpayment' refund requests.
- Remember eBay's Money Back Guarantee only covers eligible items bought and paid for through eBay.
- eBay won't email you for your bank details to 'release' a payment — treat any such message as a scam.
If you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/facebook-marketplace-scam-uk/ guide covers the same off-platform tricks elsewhere.


## 6. If you've paid or shared details

Act quickly. If you paid through eBay or by card, open a dispute with eBay (the Money Back Guarantee) and ask your bank or card issuer about a chargeback. If you sent a bank transfer off-platform, report it as fraud immediately — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. If you shared bank or card details on a fake eBay page, contact your bank using the number on your card and ask them to secure the account. Consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the listing, messages, and any receipts as evidence.


## 7. How to report an eBay scam (UK)

Report the member, listing, or message to eBay through its Help & Contact or report-a-buyer/report-an-item options, so eBay can act. If you got a suspicious 'eBay' email with a link, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk and report it to eBay. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. Keep screenshots of everything.


## FAQ

**Is eBay's buyer protection real?**

Yes — eBay's Money Back Guarantee can refund eligible items that don't arrive or don't match the listing, but only when you buy and pay through eBay. If you pay off-platform (bank transfer, gift card, 'friends and family'), that protection doesn't apply, which is exactly why scammers try to move you off eBay.

**A buyer or seller wants to deal outside eBay — is that safe?**

No. Taking the deal off eBay — paying by bank transfer or gift card, or messaging on WhatsApp — removes eBay's protection and is the most common eBay scam. Keep messages and payment on eBay, however genuine or convenient the other person sounds.

**I got an 'eBay' email asking me to confirm my bank details — is it genuine?**

No. eBay won't email you asking for your bank details, password, or card number to 'release a payment' or 'verify your account'. Don't click the link; check your account by typing ebay.co.uk yourself or using the app, and forward the email to report@phishing.gov.uk.

**A buyer overpaid and wants the difference back — what should I do?**

Don't refund anything. The overpayment is fake — the original payment will be reversed or never clears — and the 'difference' you send is real money lost. Check what has actually arrived in your account, and report the buyer to eBay.

**How do I report an eBay scam?**

Report the member or listing to eBay through Help & Contact. Forward suspicious 'eBay' emails to report@phishing.gov.uk. If you lost money, report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101).
