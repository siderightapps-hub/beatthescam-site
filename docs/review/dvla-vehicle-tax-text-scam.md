## 1. What the fake DVLA tax text says

A DVLA vehicle-tax scam text is a smishing message that pretends to come from the DVLA so you tap a link and hand over card or bank details. A typical one reads something like: 'DVLA: Our records show your latest vehicle tax payment failed. Update your details to avoid a penalty: gov.uk-vehicletax-refund.com'. Others dangle a 'tax refund' you can claim. The wording shifts — a failed payment, a refund waiting, a penalty looming — but the goal is the same: rush you onto a fake GOV.UK-style page that captures your money and details. This guide shows how to tell a fake from the real thing, how to check with the DVLA safely, and what to do if you've already paid or tapped a link.


## 2. Why a 'DVLA' text is so easy to fake

Scammers can set the sender name to read 'DVLA' or 'GOV.UK', so a fake can look official and even land near genuine messages. They send the same text to millions of UK numbers, knowing most people own or know a vehicle. The message leans on official branding, a deadline, and the fear of a fine. But the key fact cuts through all of it: the DVLA does not ask for payment or bank details by text or email, you can only tax a vehicle or get a refund through GOV.UK, and genuine tax refunds are issued automatically — you never have to 'claim' one through a link.


## 3. Signs a 'DVLA' text is a scam

- It asks you to pay, 'update payment details', or 'claim a refund' through a link.
- The link isn't gov.uk — look for lookalikes like 'gov.uk-vehicletax-refund.com' or a shortened link (the only genuine address ends in gov.uk).
- It pressures you with a penalty, a fine, or a deadline if you don't act now.
- It asks for card numbers, bank details, or personal information.
- It claims a refund is waiting — real DVLA refunds are automatic, never claimed via a text link.
- Small tells can be clues — odd spacing, a foreign number, or wording that doesn't read like official guidance — but many scam texts are polished, so a tidy message proves nothing.


## 4. How the vehicle-tax scam works

First, a text lands claiming your vehicle tax failed, is due, or that a refund is waiting. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'fix' the payment or 'claim' the money. Third, the link opens a fake GOV.UK-style page asking for your card or bank details and personal information. Fourth, anything you enter goes straight to the criminals. Fifth, they take a payment, set up further charges, or use your details for identity fraud and to target you again. Some pages take a small 'fee' to seem believable while harvesting your card details for bigger fraud later. Slowing down at the first text — and only ever using GOV.UK — stops the whole chain.


## 5. How to check with the DVLA safely (GOV.UK)

Never tap the link or call a number from the text. To check anything about your vehicle tax, go to GOV.UK by typing gov.uk into your browser yourself, then use the official 'tax your vehicle' and vehicle-enquiry services — they show your tax status instantly and for free. The DVLA will never text or email you for payment or bank details, so a message that does is a scam by definition. If you want to contact the DVLA, use only the contact details published on GOV.UK. When you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat of GOV.UK, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks.


## 6. If you paid or shared card details

Act quickly. If you entered card details or made a payment, contact your bank or card issuer straight away — use the number on the back of your card — tell them it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the transaction (a card payment may be recoverable through chargeback). If you made a bank transfer, report it as fraud immediately: since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits, exclusions and a claiming deadline. Change any password you reused, and to guard your identity consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report a DVLA text scam (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you're in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. The DVLA also asks the public to report scam messages that misuse its name, via the contact routes on GOV.UK. Keep a screenshot and a note of what happened.


## FAQ

**Does the DVLA send texts about vehicle tax?**

The DVLA does not ask for payment or bank details by text or email, and you can only tax a vehicle or sort a refund through GOV.UK. Any message asking you to pay, 'update payment details', or 'claim a refund' through a link is a scam — check your status yourself on GOV.UK instead.

**I got a 'DVLA refund' text — is it real?**

No. Genuine DVLA vehicle-tax refunds are issued automatically based on DVLA records; you never claim one through a text or email link. A 'refund waiting, click here' message is a scam designed to capture your card or bank details.

**The text came just as my tax was due — could it be genuine?**

Timing proves nothing — scammers blast these messages constantly, so some will coincide with a real renewal. Ignore the text and check your tax status directly on GOV.UK, or use the number/details published there. Never act on the link in the message.

**I entered my card details on a DVLA-style site — what now?**

Contact your bank or card issuer immediately using the number on your card, tell them it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment. Report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk, and consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk to protect your identity.

**How do I report a fake DVLA text?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider), then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). If it came by email, send it to report@phishing.gov.uk. You can also report misuse of the DVLA's name via the contacts on GOV.UK.
