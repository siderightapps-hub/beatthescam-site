## 1. What an Indeed job scam looks like

An Indeed job scam uses a real-looking listing or a direct message from a 'recruiter' to take your money or your details. A typical message reads: `Congratulations! You've been selected for a remote role paying £35/hour. To begin onboarding, message us on WhatsApp and pay a refundable £120 equipment and background-check fee.` The job may not exist, or the 'work' may be moving money or parcels for criminals. The hook is easy money and flattery; the catch is an upfront fee, a move off Indeed, or a 'task' that launders money. This guide shows how to spot a fake recruiter, check a job safely, and report it.


## 2. Why these job scams are convincing

Scammers copy real company names and post listings that look professional, then message applicants directly. They move you off Indeed to WhatsApp or Telegram quickly, where there's no oversight, and use the promise of high pay for little work to lower your guard. The single most useful rule cuts through all of it: a genuine UK employer or recruitment agency will never ask you to pay to get a job — no fee for training, equipment, a uniform, a DBS/background check, or a visa — and will never ask you to receive money and pass it on.


## 3. Signs an Indeed job is a scam

- It asks you to pay anything up front — equipment, training, a background or DBS check, a 'starter kit', or a visa.
- It moves you off Indeed to WhatsApp or Telegram almost immediately.
- The pay is very high for simple work, or you're 'hired' with no real interview.
- It asks for your bank details, ID documents, or a copy of your passport before any genuine interview or offer.
- The 'job' is completing tasks, 'optimising' apps, or receiving and forwarding money or parcels — these are task scams and money-mule recruitment.
- It overpays you and asks you to buy equipment and send back the difference.
- The company has no verifiable website, address, or Companies House record.


## 4. How the job scam works

First, you see a listing or get a message about a well-paid, often remote role. Second, the 'recruiter' moves you to WhatsApp and offers the job fast, with little or no interview. Third, they ask for an upfront fee, or set you 'tasks' that involve paying in money to 'unlock' commission. Fourth, you pay — or you receive money and forward it on, unknowingly laundering it. Finally, the fee is gone, the commission never arrives, and a money-mule role can leave you facing frozen accounts or criminal liability. Verifying the employer before you share or pay anything stops it.


## 5. How to check a job is genuine

Slow down and verify before you pay or share anything.
- Never pay to get a job — no UK employer or agency charges you for equipment, training, or a background check.
- Find the company independently: its official website's careers page, a Companies House search, or a phone number you found yourself — not one the 'recruiter' gave you.
- Keep messages on Indeed; be wary of recruiters who only use WhatsApp or Telegram.
- Don't share your bank details, passport, or ID before a genuine interview and a written offer.
- Treat any 'job' that asks you to receive and forward money as money-mule recruitment, and walk away.
If you're unsure whether a recruiter's website is genuine, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/linkedin-job-scam-guide-uk/ guide covers the same trick on LinkedIn.


## 6. If you've paid or shared details

Act quickly. If you paid a fee, contact your bank or card issuer using the number on your card, tell them it was a scam, and ask about a chargeback (card) or, for a transfer, report it as fraud — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. If you shared ID documents, consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and watch for accounts or credit taken out in your name, checking your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. If you received and forwarded money, stop immediately and tell your bank — you may have been used as a money mule, which can freeze your accounts and carry serious consequences. Keep the listing, messages, and any receipts as evidence.


## 7. How to report an Indeed job scam (UK)

Report the listing or recruiter to Indeed using the report option on the job or message, so they can remove it. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a scam job reached you by text, forward it to 7726. For free advice, contact the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133. Keep screenshots of everything.


## FAQ

**Is it normal to pay for equipment or a background check to start a job?**

No. A genuine UK employer or recruitment agency never asks you to pay to get a job — not for equipment, training, a uniform, a DBS/background check, or a visa. Any request for an upfront 'refundable' fee is a scam, no matter how official it sounds.

**Is Indeed a legitimate job site?**

Yes, Indeed is a genuine job site — but scammers post fake listings and message applicants directly. The site being real doesn't make every listing or recruiter genuine, so verify the employer independently before you share details or pay anything.

**A recruiter wants my bank details and passport before any interview — red flag?**

Yes. You don't need to share bank details or ID to apply or interview. Genuine employers collect those only after a real offer, through proper channels. Sharing them early risks identity theft, so verify the company first.

**I've already paid a recruiter — can I get my money back?**

Possibly. If you paid by card, ask your bank about a chargeback. If you sent a bank transfer, report it as fraud at once: since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. Report the listing to Indeed and the scam to Report Fraud, and keep all the messages.

**How do I report an Indeed job scam?**

Report the listing or recruiter to Indeed, then report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). If you were asked to move money for a 'job', tell your bank immediately.
