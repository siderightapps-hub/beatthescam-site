## 1. What a fake council tax text looks like

A council tax scam text is a smishing message that pretends to come from your council so you tap a link and hand over bank, card, or personal details. A typical one reads: `COUNCIL TAX: You are due a refund of £167.40. Claim now: counciltax-refund.example`, while another threatens arrears, bailiffs, or court unless you 'pay' through a link. Council tax is run by your local council, not HMRC — so a genuine refund or bill comes from your own council, not a random text. This guide shows how to tell a fake from a real council message, how to check safely, and how to report it.


## 2. Why these council tax texts are convincing

Scammers spoof the sender so a message reads as 'Council Tax' or your council's name, and they send the same text to huge numbers of people, knowing almost everyone pays council tax. They use the pull of a refund or the fear of arrears and court action. The key facts cut through it: your council won't ask you to claim a refund or pay arrears through a link in a text, genuine refunds are arranged through your own council (often automatically or via its website), and real arrears are dealt with by letter and your council's official process — never a 'pay now or face court' text.


## 3. Signs a council tax text is a scam

- It offers a council tax refund or rebate you can claim by tapping a link.
- It threatens arrears, bailiffs, or court unless you pay immediately through a link.
- The link isn't your council's official site or gov.uk — it's a lookalike or shortened address.
- It asks for your bank details, card number, or personal information to 'process' a refund or payment.
- It can't tell you which council it's from, or the amount doesn't match your real bill.
- Errors can be clues, but a tidy, official-looking text can still be a scam.


## 4. How the council tax scam works

First, a text claims you're owed a refund or that you owe council tax. Second, it pushes you to a link to 'claim' the money or 'pay' before a deadline. Third, the link opens a fake council or GOV.UK-style page that asks for your bank or card details. Fourth, the criminals capture them. Fifth, they take a payment, set up further charges, or use your details for identity fraud. Because a refund feels welcome and a court threat feels urgent, people act fast — so checking only through your real council breaks the chain.


## 5. How to check with your council safely

Don't tap the link or call a number from the text.
- Find your council's official website yourself at gov.uk/find-local-council, and use its council tax pages.
- Use the phone number or contact details on a real council tax bill or your council's site.
- Remember a genuine refund is arranged by your council directly, not claimed through a texted link.
- If a text mentions arrears, check your account through your council, not the message.
If you're unsure whether a linked site is a copycat, our guide on /guides/is-this-website-a-scam/ walks through the checks, and our /guides/dvla-vehicle-tax-text-scam/ guide covers the same trick using other government names.


## 6. If you clicked or shared details

Act quickly. If you entered card details or paid, contact your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card, tell them it was a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute the payment (a card payment may be recoverable through chargeback). If you made a bank transfer, report it as fraud immediately — since 7 October 2024, most APP (authorised push-payment) fraud victims can claim reimbursement for UK transfers made over Faster Payments or CHAPS, subject to limits and exclusions — the PSR rules include a 13-month claim window, a maximum claim of £85,000, possible exclusions, and a possible excess of up to £100. If you shared personal details, consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk and check your credit report with Experian, Equifax or TransUnion. Tell your council too, so they're aware. Keep the text as evidence.


## 7. How to report a council tax scam text (UK)

Forward the text to 7726 — it is free, and it reports the message to your mobile provider, which can investigate and take action. Report the scam to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 if you are in England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, report to Police Scotland on 101. If a similar scam reached you by email, forward it to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. Let your council know through its official website, and keep a screenshot.


## FAQ

**Does my council send texts about council tax?**

Some councils do send genuine reminders by text, so a council text isn't automatically a scam — but your council won't ask you to claim a refund or pay arrears through a link in a text, or hand over bank details that way. Check anything about your account through your council's official website instead.

**Is council tax run by HMRC?**

No. Council tax is run by your local council, not HMRC. A genuine refund or bill comes from your own council, and you can find its official contact details at gov.uk/find-local-council. (HMRC deals with things like income tax and tax refunds — a separate scam covered in our HMRC guide.)

**I got a 'council tax refund' text — is it real?**

Treat it as a scam until you've checked. Genuine council tax refunds are arranged by your council directly, not claimed through a text link. Don't tap the link; go to your council's official website (find it at gov.uk/find-local-council) and check your account there.

**I entered my card details on a council tax page — what now?**

Contact your bank or card issuer immediately using the number on your card, report it as a scam, and ask them to stop the card and dispute any payment. Then report the scam to Report Fraud and consider a Cifas Protective Registration at cifas.org.uk to protect your identity.

**How do I report a council tax scam text?**

Forward it to 7726 (free; it reports the message to your mobile provider), then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales or Northern Ireland; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). Tell your council too, through its official website.
